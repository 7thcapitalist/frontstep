# Frontstep visual quality bar

Nine QA-approved niche-family examples are indexed in `examples/catalog.json`. They are locally authored starter systems for the same niche family. Reuse a proven layout or interaction when it suits a new brief; replace the business identity, facts, imagery and credits with validated current material. These examples passed functional QA, not a universal visual rating. Judge visual craft separately from real-business readiness; disclosed synthetic content is legitimate. Use VISUAL-RUBRIC.md for the independent weighted score, which must be strictly above 9.0.

For every build:

1. Give the first screen an ownable idea tied to the business and an obvious next action. A generic split hero, card grid, and neutral stock portrait do not meet this bar.
2. Use the actual images as composition material. Check their subject, crop, lighting, orientation, and visual consistency before deciding their role. If no credible image exists, make type, spatial rhythm, or a useful interface carry the design. Never fabricate photographic evidence.
3. Design at least three distinct section rhythms. Avoid a page that repeats the same card or rule pattern from top to bottom. Keep mobile reading order intentional.
4. Give the premium feature a visible, contextual response after each action. Test an empty state, a populated state, removal or correction, and the action summary. Do not rely on a status that is only exposed to screen readers.
5. Name one specific motion idea in the plan and implement it where it helps understanding. Include a still state and `prefers-reduced-motion` treatment. Scroll reveals must not leave content invisible if JavaScript fails.
6. Render screenshots before calling the site ready. Keep images and content visible without JavaScript. Inspect viewport captures and a scroll storyboard as well as a full-page capture; a still capture before a scroll transition is not evidence of a missing asset. Check desktop and 390px mobile for clipping, whitespace, contrast, and caption legibility.
7. Inspect images at full resolution. Visible licensed-stock credits and honest illustrative captions matter, especially for a synthetic demo. Do not imply a stock person is the business owner or a stock dish is the restaurant's dish.
8. Treat browser QA as a minimum. A passing feature test does not establish strong visual design; the independent visual review can still fail and should produce concrete edits.
9. Give the page one recognizable graphic gesture that belongs to this business: a shape, typographic treatment, framing device or transition with a reason tied to the brief. Carry it through the hero, one later section and the feature state, with restraint. A neutral split hero plus alternating tinted rectangles is still a template even when the copy is specific.
10. In a mobile feature capture, keep the changed value and next action near the selection. Avoid large empty reserved blocks, sticky elements that cover controls, and long repeated instructions between feedback and action. Enabled actions need strong contrast at every animation frame.
